#+begin_abstract
A fold that can still see the tree it is consuming, and an unfold that can
stop expanding and graft a finished subtree in place. Paramorphism and
apomorphism are the first proper duals in the catalog, and their carriers
--- ~std::pair~ and a project ~either~ --- are the product and coproduct
the rest of the series is built from.

* para: A Fold That Remembers the Original

~fold_fix~ has amnesia by design.
By the time the algebra sees an ~Add~, the children are already strings or
ints; what they /were/ is gone.
Usually that is the point.
Sometimes it is the problem: a pretty-printer that wants minimal
parentheses needs each child's rendered string *and* its original shape ---
parenthesize the rendering only if the original was an ~Add~ under a
~Mul~.

The paramorphism [cite:@meertens1992paramorphisms] hands the algebra both.
From [[file:../../src/smd/fixpoint/para.hpp][=src/smd/fixpoint/para.hpp=]]:

#+transclude: [[file:../../src/smd/fixpoint/para.hpp::5cdab85f-88b0-477e-9da4-6766f7615a74]] :lines 2- :src cpp :end "5cdab85f-88b0-477e-9da4-6766f7615a74 end"

The delta against ~fold_fix~ is one lambda: instead of mapping children to
~Result~, it maps them to ~std::pair<Fix<F>, Result>~ --- the original
subtree first, the fold result second.
The algebra's argument type ~F<pair<Fix<F>, Result>>~ says exactly what is
available at each child: everything.

The pretty-printer, from
[[file:../../src/examples/para_pretty_print.cpp][=src/examples/para_pretty_print.cpp=]]:

#+transclude: [[file:../../src/examples/para_pretty_print.cpp::bcdeef00-3342-4245-aca3-f4a63d366637]] :lines 2- :src cpp :end "bcdeef00-3342-4245-aca3-f4a63d366637 end"

The ~Mul~ case is where it pays off: ~is_add(child.first)~ interrogates the
original subtree; ~child.second~ is the already-rendered string it wraps
or passes through.
The program prints ~2 * 3 + 4~ for one association and ~2 * (3 + 4)~ for
the other --- parentheses where meaning requires them, nowhere
else.

Degeneracy law: ignore ~.first~ everywhere and ~para~ /is/ ~fold_fix~.
The test suite states that literally, and every scheme from here on has a
law of the same shape --- forget the extra power, recover the simpler
scheme.

* apo: An Unfold That Can Stop Early

Now dualize.
~para~'s algebra receives, alongside each result, the original subtree ---
a product.
~apo~'s coalgebra returns, at each child position, /either/ a finished
subtree to graft /or/ a seed to keep unfolding --- a coproduct
[cite:@uustalu1999primitive] [cite:@vene2000categorical].
From [[file:../../src/smd/fixpoint/apo.hpp][=src/smd/fixpoint/apo.hpp=]]:

#+transclude: [[file:../../src/smd/fixpoint/apo.hpp::6956f079-e2ee-4707-9d9e-79082548afef]] :lines 2- :src cpp :end "6956f079-e2ee-4707-9d9e-79082548afef end"

The worker ~match~-es each child: Left returns its subtree as-is --- no
recursion --- and Right recurses like ~unfold_fix~.
Note what the doc comment promises about references: a coalgebra may
return ~either<const Fix<F>&, Seed>~, and the graft copies exactly once,
at the graft point.
The zero-copy graft falls out of ~match~ never asking which instantiation
it holds.

The motivating example is ordered insertion into a sorted list.
Unfold the output list element by element while the input's head is
smaller; the moment the insertion point appears, emit the new value and
graft the /entire untouched remainder/ with Left.
From [[file:../../src/examples/apo_sorted_insert.cpp][=src/examples/apo_sorted_insert.cpp=]]:

#+transclude: [[file:../../src/examples/apo_sorted_insert.cpp::e0098ed1-a5bf-4681-8dfb-8cd06ee73e5d]] :lines 2- :src cpp :end "e0098ed1-a5bf-4681-8dfb-8cd06ee73e5d end"

A plain ~unfold_fix~ cannot express "and the rest is what it already was"
--- it must walk to the end, rebuilding.
~apo~ says it in one ~make_left~.

* either: The Sum the Library Actually Wanted

That Left/Right vocabulary is a project type, and it exists because the
obvious candidate failed.
From [[file:../../src/smd/typeclass/either.hpp][=src/smd/typeclass/either.hpp=]]:

#+transclude: [[file:../../src/smd/typeclass/either.hpp::8cd8e50e-209b-4b8b-8979-06952416c53e]] :lines 2- :src cpp :end "8cd8e50e-209b-4b8b-8979-06952416c53e end"

~std::expected~ was considered and rejected, and the decision log is
specific.
Its sides are asymmetric: the error side needs the ~unexpected~ wrapper,
error-side mapping is spelled differently, and above all
~expected<T, T>~ --- which arises /naturally/ here, an apo whose Seed is
itself ~Fix<F>~, as in the insert above --- forces tag-only construction
and ambushes generic code.
~either~ makes ~Left~ and ~Right~ distinct wrapper types on *both* sides,
so ~either<T, T>~ constructs and compares without ceremony.

The side convention is fixed once, library-wide, matching the Haskell
sources: *Left stops, Right continues*.
Apo: Left = graft this subtree.
Elgot (Part 10): Left = here is the final answer.
The monad instance is right-biased, ~pure~ is ~make_right~, and Part 11
will lean on that when ~apo~ is recovered from the generalized
unfold.

One more symmetry, now: the design treats ~std::pair~ and
~either~ as proper duals --- product and coproduct --- with matched
vocabulary on each side: ~fanout~ builds toward a pair as ~fanin~ matches
away from an either, and the value slot sits second in both (~pair~'s
env-comonad ~extract~ is ~.second~; ~either~'s monadic ~pure~ is
~make_right~).
~para~ rides the pair; ~apo~ rides the either.
The duality is not decoration --- in Part 11 it becomes the actual
mechanism, when ~dist_para~ (pair side) and ~dist_apo~ (either side) sit
in the same slot of ~gcata~ and ~gana~ respectively.
